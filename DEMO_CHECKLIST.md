# Demo checklist — build-in-public demos (RET-5)

A standing document, not code. A demo is VYUK doing a real voice
task on a real machine, shown to the public. Every item below is
checked before a demo is recorded, and the check is recorded with
the demo. If any item fails, the demo does not happen.

## Before recording

- [ ] **Per-demo yes, recorded first.** The owner said yes to THIS
      demo, in words, before recording started. No standing blanket
      consent — it is his screen, his data, his voice. The yes and
      its date are written down with the demo (who asked, what was
      asked, what he said).
- [ ] **Real machine, real task.** The demo runs on his Windows
      machine, doing a real task he actually wants done — not a
      staged script, not a lab fixture, not a mock.
- [ ] **Nothing sensitive on screen.** Nothing on screen shows a
      secret, a key, a password, a token, a full file path, a
      contact, or another person's data. Check the desktop, the
      open windows, the notification area, and the taskbar before
      recording. If something sensitive is visible, hide it or
      pick another moment — never record through it.

## While recording

- [ ] **The whole task, uncut.** The recording shows the task
      from the request to the result, including every confirmation
      popup and every failure. Nothing is cut, skipped, or sped
      past. A pause to wait for a confirmation is part of the demo.
- [ ] **The voice loop's field verdict is the subject, never the
      assumption.** The demo is evidence about whether the loop
      works on his machine — it does not assume it works and then
      perform around it.

## After recording

- [ ] **Never lab green as field proof.** Lab results (the
      neighbour suites, the provers, the gap scripts) are lab
      results. A demo must never be captioned, titled, or
      described as proof of something that only passed in the lab.
      What the demo shows is what the demo shows — nothing more.
- [ ] **A demo that fails is published as a failure with the
      reason, or not at all.** A failed demo is never edited into
      a success: no cutting the failure out, no re-recording the
      failed part and splicing it in, no caption that hides it.
      If the failure is not worth publishing as a failure, the
      demo stays unpublished.
- [ ] **No charging, no paywall, no price.** Nothing in or around
      the demo sells anything or promises a price (ruling 34 is
      open).

## What a demo never is

- Not a recording of a lab run. Not a screen recording of a test
  suite passing. Not a highlight reel with the confirmations and
  failures cut out. Not a demo recorded by anyone but the owner
  on his machine — a demo recorded by anyone else is not field
  proof.
